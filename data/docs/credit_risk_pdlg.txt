Credit risk modeling breaks expected loss into three components, together known as PD, LGD, and EAD. Expected loss equals PD times LGD times EAD.

PD is the probability of default: the likelihood that a borrower fails to repay within a given horizon, usually one year. Banks estimate PD with statistical models, most commonly logistic regression on borrower characteristics such as credit score, income, debt-to-income ratio, and payment history. The model outputs a score that is calibrated into a probability.

LGD is loss given default: the share of the exposure the lender actually loses if default happens. A mortgage has low LGD because the house can be sold to recover most of the loan; an unsecured credit card has high LGD. EAD is exposure at default: the amount outstanding when default occurs, which for revolving credit includes an estimate of how much of the unused line the borrower will draw down first.

Regulators care deeply about these models. Under the Basel framework, banks using the internal ratings-based approach must estimate PD, LGD, and EAD themselves and hold capital against the results. Supervisors validate the models under guidance like SR 11-7, which demands conceptual soundness, ongoing monitoring, and independent validation.
